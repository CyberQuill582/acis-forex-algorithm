## 0. MISSION

Merge three algorithmic subsystems — the **QIEA** (Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithm) parameter optimizer, the **NVMA** (Non-linear Volatility-regime & Momentum Adapter) regime engine, and **AGON** (Greek ἀγών, *the contest* — né Alpha Arena), the multi-strategy signal arena — into one self-governing capital organism that:

1. Generates consistent positive risk-adjusted returns across instruments, asset classes, and market regimes.
2. Governs its own capital allocation to prevent edge decay at scale (marginal-alpha law, §6).
3. Detects and adapts to regime shifts in real time.
4. Enforces hard risk guardrails that no performance pressure, module, or config flag can override.
5. Fragments and randomises execution footprint to avoid predictable statistical patterns.
6. Diversifies across instruments/timeframes to cap correlation risk — and **trades the relationships themselves**: historically coupled opposite-moving pairs are a first-class edge (divergence/convergence, §5.5), with exposure always computed net of correlation so a "hedged" book can never silently be a doubled bet.
7. Operates autonomously during live sessions — no human, and **no LLM**, in the execution path.
8. Decides on thorough, no-nonsense information **including news**: a complete, timestamped event picture from trusted channels only (§3.5) — and **never trades on news through a platform whose rules forbid it** (the platform-rules law, §3.5/§6). Prepares for black swans by construction (§8.5), not by hope.
9. Grounds its strategies in the recognized trading literature: published strategies by prominent practitioners are implemented faithfully and tested **raw** first; bespoke strategies are derived from that thinking for today's market structure, with declared lineage and falsifiers — never by parameter-mining historical data into a flattering win rate (§5.6). The hunt never stops: literature acquisition and re-validation run on a standing cadence (§5.7).
10. **Attaches and survives** — runs seriously on whatever account it is given, personally funded or prop-firm, on any supported platform: the account's own rulebook (drawdown as *the platform* measures it, consistency rules, lot caps, min hold, weekend bans, news restrictions) is imported at attach time and enforced as hard constraints alongside ACIS's own guardrails, always stricter-of (§9.5 account doctrine). Blowing the account by violating a rule the platform published is an unforgivable engineering failure, not bad luck.
11. **Rigged for evolution** — markets drift; the engine's edge is re-earned, never assumed. Every strategy and parameter set carries a re-validation due date; stale edge is demoted down the rung ladder automatically; retired strategies wait in a graveyard with declared revival conditions (§5.7). Nothing in the architecture calcifies: new instruments, platforms, and strategies attach through declared contracts (§3.7, §7.5), never by editing core layers.

Optimisation target: **risk-adjusted alpha that survives scale.** A system that loses no money but makes no money has failed. Every design decision must answer: *"does this make the system more likely to survive a regime it has never seen?"* If no — redesign.

**The LLM boundary (binding):** LLMs (via Phronesis or a human session) may analyse logs, propose threshold/config changes, and review performance — out-of-band, through §12 tools, with changes staged like QIEA params (§5). LLM output never sizes, signals, routes, or halts a trade in real time. The fast loop is deterministic Python only.

**The control boundary (binding):** ACIS is operated and supervised by the **Phronesis agent harness** — the orchestrator of record — exclusively through the §12 MCP contract (`acis_halt` / `acis_flatten` / `acis_resume` / `acis_set_mode` / `acis_stage_config` plus the read-only resources). Phronesis reasons *about* ACIS out-of-band; it never enters the fast loop, and its reasoning is not a trading input (the LLM boundary above still holds). The safety asymmetry binds it like every client: Phronesis — or any agent — may halt or flatten; only a human token may resume or change mode (§12). Any compliant MCP client can stand in for Phronesis, and nothing in ACIS depends on Phronesis's internals — the seam is the contract, not the harness.

## 2. LAYER COMMUNICATION LAW (architectural constitution)

Seven layers, one responsibility each. Communication flows **downward only**, one layer at a time, with exactly one exception: the Risk Monitor's upward HALT channel.

```
DATA INGESTION            → MarketState
NVMA REGIME ENGINE        → RegimeContext
QIEA OPTIMIZER            → ActiveParamSet
AGON (SIGNAL ARENA)       → Signal
CAPITAL GOVERNOR          → SizedOrder
EXECUTION ORCHESTRATOR    → FillReport
RISK MONITOR              → HALT ↑ (to Capital Governor ONLY)
```

No layer imports a layer above it. No layer bypasses the layer directly below it. Enforce with module-level import guards plus an import-linter contract in `tests/unit/` that fails the build on violation. Objects crossing a boundary are frozen dataclasses — consumers never mutate producer state.

Conflict resolution priority (highest wins): 1) Risk Monitor HALT · 2) NVMA `tradeable=False` veto · 3) Governor hard-limit override · 4) Signal expiry · 5) QIEA pending-param promotion.

| Conflict | Resolution |
|---|---|
| NVMA no-trade + AGON high-conviction signal | Discard signal. No exception. |
| QIEA new params ready + open positions | Stage; deploy at next session boundary only |
| New signal would breach correlation-cluster cap | Governor sizes to zero. Do not split. |
| Daily drawdown limit hit while sizing | Halt new positions for session |
| Regime shift + no evolved params for new regime | `tradeable=False` until QIEA completes evolution |
| Two strategies signal opposite directions, same instrument | Discard both. Ambiguity = no trade. |

## 3. DATA INGESTION `[DIR:core/data]`

`MarketStateFactory` produces validated, frozen `MarketState` objects:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class MarketState:
    timestamp: datetime; instrument: str
    bid: float; ask: float; spread: float; mid: float; volume: float
    order_book_depth: Dict[str, List[Tuple[float, float]]]  # {"bids": [(px,vol)], "asks": [...]}
    tick_velocity: float          # ticks/sec, rolling 10s
    session: str                  # london | new_york | tokyo | overlap
    macro_events_imminent: bool   # Tier-1 event within 30 min
    raw: Dict                     # original feed payload
```

Requirements: validate every field on construction, raise `DataQualityError` on invalid input (no silent coercion); flag stale data (timestamp delta > configurable threshold) — stale ⇒ downstream treats market as untradeable for that instrument; feed connectors are the **feed faces of the §7.5 platform adapters** — OANDA v20 (primary, per Master Spec), MT5, and a mock paper feed with identical interface in v1; further platforms (IBKR, cTrader, DXtrade, TradeLocker, Match-Trader, futures gateways) attach through the same contract, never by modifying this layer; every MarketState logged to structured JSONL with microsecond timestamps. Storage: SQLite in dev, **TimescaleDB** hypertables in production; the writer is async and out-of-band — a slow DB never blocks the fast loop (bounded queue, drop-oldest with a logged `DATA_BACKPRESSURE` event).

### 3.5 EVENT & NEWS INTAKE `[DIR:core/data]` — deterministic, trusted channels only

`events.py` builds the **EventPicture** that backs `MarketState.macro_events_imminent` and everything news-aware downstream. This is data plumbing, not judgment — it stays inside the LLM boundary (§0).

**The untrusted-input law (binding, prompt-injection defence):** every external headline, calendar note, and feed payload is **content to classify, never an instruction to obey**. It is parsed and matched against the deterministic keyword/taxonomy classifier and is never interpolated into a control decision, a tool call, or (when an out-of-band LLM later reads it, §12) a prompt as though it were a command. A feed that carries text like *"ignore the blackout"* is data that gets logged and classified — it changes no control flow. Two independent enforcement layers (events + Governor override, §6) still stand; no string in a payload can weaken either. Mirrors the sibling engines' untrusted-input rule (PROKOPE §3, KALLOS §3, SYNEDRION §3).

- **Trusted channels only**, declared in `config/event_sources.yaml` with a trust tier per source: economic calendars (scheduled events with tier, expected/actual/previous), central-bank release schedules, and a small vetted breaking-headline feed (tier/affected-currencies classification is keyword/taxonomy-based and deterministic — no LLM). Adding or re-tiering a source is a human config commit, never a runtime action. Reputable programmatic calendars are the model for what "trusted" means here — **examples, not a hardcode or an endorsement**; the real list lives in `event_sources.yaml` by human commit: Forex Factory and FXStreet for cross-verifiable scheduled events and the Trading Economics API where programmatic access with consensus/actual/previous is wanted. The **Tier-1 taxonomy** that drives blackout logic is the set that actually moves FX and that prop desks restrict around: Non-Farm Payrolls, CPI, central-bank rate decisions / FOMC, and GDP. Observed prop-firm blackout windows run ~2–5 min either side of a Tier-1 release — `platform_rules.yaml` (§9) carries the exact per-account number; this layer only *computes* windows from it, never invents one.
- **Calendar integrity:** Tier-1 event times cross-checked against ≥2 sources; disagreement ⇒ take the earlier time and log `CALENDAR_DISAGREEMENT`. All event times normalized to UTC with explicit DST handling — a calendar shifting one hour against broker bars is a classic silent killer (§13 glitch classes).
- **Blackout windows:** for the active broker/account, `platform_rules.yaml` (§9) declares whether and how news trading is restricted (e.g., prop-firm "no open/close ±2 min around Tier-1 events"). `events.py` computes the concrete per-instrument blackout windows; the Governor enforces them as a hard override (§6). Two independent layers must both fail for a rule breach to occur. **The platform-rules law (binding): where the platform forbids trading on news, no signal, conviction, or config flag can open a position in a blackout window. Where the platform allows it, news-driven regimes are tradeable like any other.**
- **Feed-outage fail-safe:** if the calendar feed is stale beyond threshold, the system assumes unknown-event risk: `macro_events_imminent = true` for Tier-1-scheduled hours known from the last good sync, and new-position blackout on rule-restricted accounts. Unknown never degrades to "no event."

### 3.6 GEOPOLITICAL & UNSCHEDULED EVENTS `[DIR:core/data]` — the world beyond the calendar

Scheduled releases (§3.5) are not the only trend-changers: wars and escalations, ceasefires, sanctions packages, trade deals sealed between nations, elections, supply shocks. These are **Tier-G events** — unscheduled, narrative-driven, regime-changing. ACIS takes them seriously through two lanes with very different powers:

- **Fast lane (deterministic, in the loop):** the vetted headline feed (§3.5) is classified by a deterministic keyword/taxonomy matcher extended with a **Tier-G taxonomy** — conflict/escalation, ceasefire/peace-deal, sanctions, trade-agreement, government-collapse/election-shock, supply-shock — each taxonomy entry mapping to affected currencies/commodities in `event_sources.yaml` (e.g. escalation → CHF/JPY/XAU risk-off complex; OPEC supply shock → energies + CAD). A Tier-G match sets a shock flag consumed by NVMA (§4) exactly like `macro_events_imminent`.
- **The shock-asymmetry law (binding):** in the fast loop, an unscheduled event can only ever make the engine **more conservative** — raise the effective volatility class, veto tradeability, tighten the Governor's shock fraction (§8.5). It never opens a position, never sets a direction, never sizes up. ACIS does not trade the headline; it trades the *price behaviour* the headline causes, through the regime engine, where its evolved edge actually lives. This keeps the untrusted-input law (§3.5) airtight: the most a crafted headline can ever achieve is to make ACIS trade less.
- **Shock flags are bounded in duration (closes GAP-0015):** every Tier-G flag carries a **TTL** declared on its taxonomy entry in `event_sources.yaml` (config-owned, human-committed, per-class defaults); fresh confirmations re-arm it, expiry clears it with a logged `TIERG_EXPIRED` event and the instrument returns to ordinary regime classification — where NVMA's organic vetoes (spread, liquidity, confidence) remain sovereign throughout. Conservatism from a headline is therefore bounded by construction: a stale or hostile wire can never hold the book closed indefinitely, and Tier-G skip-time is visible in the §10 per-gate attribution.
- **Tier-G flatten (ratified by the owner, 2026-07-15 — closes GAP-0011):** if a Tier-G match (i) hits a taxonomy entry a human has committed as `flatten_eligible: true`, (ii) is independently confirmed by **≥2 trusted sources** within the taxonomy entry's confirmation window, and (iii) an affected instrument has open positions — the **Risk Monitor** flattens that instrument's positions and halts the instrument, under standard §8 halt semantics (resume human-only, §12). Anything less — single source, non-eligible entry, no confirmation — stays veto-only under the shock-asymmetry law. The injection surface stays bounded by construction: the worst a fabricated, two-source-confirmed headline can achieve is a logged, human-reviewed exit to safety — never a position, a direction, or a size.
- **Slow lane (out-of-band, LLM-side):** the full narrative spectrum — analysis of a developing war, the likely FX consequences of a signed trade deal, structural sanction regimes — is Phronesis's job through §12 (`geopolitical-brief` prompt), reading the EventPicture and world news *as content* (§12 untrusted-input law). Its conclusions land only as staged config (§9 staged-promotion path): watchlist changes, Tier-G taxonomy additions, cluster-membership proposals, per-instrument caution multipliers — at session boundaries, schema-validated, never touching guardrails. **Caution multipliers are dampen-only (closes GAP-0016): clamped to (0, 1] at the `acis_stage_config` schema level** — a patch that would raise exposure on any instrument is rejected exactly like a guardrail path, so the slow lane inherits the shock-asymmetry law: no text an LLM read can ever become a size-up. Raising exposure back to normal happens only by the multiplier's own staged expiry/removal, which restores 1.0 — never exceeds it. The LLM boundary (§0) is untouched: no LLM output ever reaches the fast loop in real time.
- Taxonomy and affected-instrument maps grow by human config commit, seeded and refreshed via the slow lane. A Tier-G feed outage fails safe exactly like §3.5: unknown never degrades to "no event."

### 3.7 ASSET-CLASS LAW `[DIR:core/data]` — one engine, many markets

ACIS's layers are asset-class-agnostic by construction: everything instrument-specific is **declared data, never code branches**. `instruments.yaml` (§9) carries, per instrument, the full contract metadata: asset class (fx | metal | index_cfd | energy | future), quote/base currency or contract currency, tick size and tick value, lot/contract step and minimums, margin model, swap/financing convention, and the **market calendar** it trades on (24/5 forex rollover, exchange sessions with holidays and half-days for futures, metals' daily break). Binding consequences:

- **Session boundaries are per-calendar (C1 generalised):** the C1 `session_boundaries_utc` list remains the forex default; every non-forex instrument resolves session boundaries, session tagging, and QIEA staging windows against its own declared calendar. One rule, many calendars — there is still no implicit boundary anywhere.
- **Futures are first-class, not forex with a costume:** contract expiry and rollover are explicit lifecycle events — an open position approaching its declared roll window is surfaced to the Governor (roll or flatten by policy, never silently held into expiry); backtests use back-adjusted continuous-contract stitching with the stitch method declared; limit-up/limit-down and exchange halts are liquidity states (§4 ILLIQUID), not errors.
- **An instrument without complete contract metadata is not tradeable** — attach-time fatal, same as a missing platform rulebook (§9). Indicator periods, ATR-based stops, and cost models all consume declared tick/contract values; a hardcoded pip assumption anywhere is a bug.
- New asset classes require only: metadata schema coverage, a market calendar, §13 glitch tests for the class's failure modes, and a platform adapter that quotes it (§7.5). Crypto and cash equities remain out of scope in v1 (§17).

## 4. NVMA REGIME ENGINE `[DIR:core/regime]` — the sovereign gatekeeper

Consumes MarketState, emits `RegimeContext` on a 60–300s cycle:

```python
@dataclass(frozen=True)
class RegimeContext:
    regime: RegimeType; trend_direction: int          # -1|0|+1
    volatility_class: VolClass                        # LOW|MEDIUM|HIGH|EXTREME
    liquidity_state: LiqState                         # DEEP|NORMAL|THIN|ILLIQUID
    tradeable: bool                                   # master veto flag
    regime_stability_score: float; estimated_half_life_seconds: int
    confidence: float; timestamp: datetime
```

12 regimes: STRONG_TREND_UP/DOWN, WEAK_TREND_UP/DOWN, RANGING_TIGHT/WIDE, BREAKOUT_IMMINENT, POST_BREAKOUT, MEAN_REVERTING, MACRO_EVENT†, ILLIQUID†, UNCLASSIFIED† († always `tradeable=False`).

Classification inputs: ATR(14/50/200) ratios → volatility class; ADX(14) → trend strength (ADX **alone** — the sole trend-strength input); Bollinger-width percentile (rolling 100) → ranging; spread deviation >2σ from session median → ILLIQUID; Hurst exponent (rolling 200, Anis–Lloyd-corrected multi-window R/S): **anti-persistence gate only — H < `hurst_meanrev` (re-derived 0.35) → mean-reverting.** *(v2.12 correction, owner-ratified: R/S Hurst is drift-invariant — a strong price trend and a random walk produce identical H to machine epsilon — so "H>0.55 trending" was a category error about what the statistic measures and is retired; Hurst can detect increment anti-persistence, never a trend.)* Macro calendar: Tier-1 event within 30 min → MACRO_EVENT; Tier-G shock flag (§3.6) on an affected instrument → MACRO_EVENT for that instrument (the shock-asymmetry law: a headline can only ever make ACIS more conservative); exchange halt / limit-lock (§3.7) → ILLIQUID.

Veto (`tradeable=False`) additionally when: confidence < 0.55; spread > 3× session median; tick velocity < 0.5× session median (dead market).

On regime shift: log full context → notify param library → if no evolved params for the new regime, `tradeable=False` until QIEA evolution completes. NVMA never triggers execution; it writes to `regime_store` only.

## 5. QIEA OPTIMIZER `[DIR:core/optimizer]`

Quantum-inspired binary chromosome evolution maintaining a **regime-indexed parameter library** (best `ParamSet` per RegimeType).

**Critical deployment rule (non-overridable by any module or config):** QIEA never modifies live parameters. Evolution runs only when market is closed OR at session boundaries with `open_positions == 0`. New params go to `PendingParamStore`; promotion to active happens only at the next valid session boundary.

**C1 — "session boundary" is defined, not assumed.** A session boundary is a discrete UTC instant enumerated in `system_config.yaml` (`session_boundaries_utc`, default: 22:00 UTC daily forex rollover plus each of the London / New York / Tokyo opens). The QIEA staging rule, the "next valid session boundary" promotion trigger, and the `session` field on `MarketState` all resolve against this one list — there is no implicit or per-instrument notion of a boundary anywhere else in the system. A bad or empty list is fatal at startup (§9).

**C2 — promotion liveness (the deadlock is observable, the safety rule is not weakened).** Because forex is 24/5, a position can straddle successive boundaries, so `open_positions == 0` may not hold at any boundary for an extended period. The safety rule is absolute and unchanged: **params are never promoted while any position is open, and never mid-session — position close is NOT a promotion trigger.** To prevent silent staleness, staged params carry `staged_at` and a `boundaries_skipped` counter; each boundary that passes with open positions increments it and logs `PARAM_PROMOTION_STALLED{regime, boundaries_skipped, staged_at}`. When `boundaries_skipped ≥ config.param_stall_alert_boundaries` (default 3) the condition is surfaced on `acis://params/pending` (§12) for orchestrator/human attention. The system waits — it never force-promotes.

`ParamSet` fields and ranges (minimum set — extend only with spec edit): ema_fast 5–50, ema_slow 20–200, rsi_period 7–21, rsi_overbought 65–80, rsi_oversold 20–35, atr_multiplier_sl 1.0–3.5, atr_multiplier_tp 1.5–5.0, momentum_lookback 5–50, breakout_atr_threshold 0.5–2.0, mean_rev_zscore_entry 1.5–2.5, max_holding_periods 1–48, plus regime, fitness_score, evolved_at.

Fitness = Sharpe×0.40 + WinRate×0.20 + ProfitFactor×0.20 + MaxDrawdownPenalty×(−0.20), evaluated on the last **500 bars of regime-labelled historical data for the target regime only** (never full history). QIEA reads historical data only — never live portfolio state.

**C8 — fitness is net of costs (binding).** The fitness mini-backtest must apply the **same** spread + commission + swap/slippage cost model used by the §13 backtest harness (`ACIS-09`) — the shared model is unit-tested identical in both places. Optimising on gross (mid-price) returns selects parameters that look profitable in a frictionless world and die net of costs; it is forbidden. This is the classic way a backtest lies, closed at the optimizer, not only at the final gate.

**C9 — positive expectancy is a hard gate (binding).** A ParamSet whose net expectancy `E = winRate·avgWin − lossRate·avgLoss` (costs included, C8) is ≤ 0 over its fitness window **cannot win evolution**, regardless of its Sharpe or win-rate components. Win rate is a *component*, never the objective: a high win rate paired with negative expectancy (tight take-profit, wide stop) is a money-loser, not an edge. The exact expectancy normalisation is a testable contract owned by `ACIS-03` (like the C6 marginal-alpha contract). This is the optimizer-side of the §5.6 anti-self-deception law — it tightens selection and weakens no guardrail.

### 5.5 AGON — THE SIGNAL ARENA `[DIR:core/signals]` (né Alpha Arena, renamed 2026-07-15)

Fast loop (tick–5s). Reads RegimeContext + ActiveParamSet → emits `Signal{signal_id, instrument, direction, conviction 0–1, entry_price, stop_loss, take_profit, strategy_type, regime_at_generation, param_set_id, generated_at, valid_for_seconds}`. Signals expire; the Governor discards expired ones.

**The reflex law (binding — owner-directed, anti-paralysis at the source):** AGON is a **reflex, not a committee**. Its strategy modules fire **independently and in parallel** — there is no discussion, no voting, no consensus round, no confidence negotiation, and no agent deliberation of any kind in the signal path (any such loop would violate the §0 LLM boundary anyway, and is exactly the analysis-paralysis §10 exists to kill). Conviction is a per-module deterministic formula, never a negotiated quantity; disagreement between modules is not debated but resolved instantly by the fixed §2 conflict table (opposite directions ⇒ discard both — ambiguity = no trade); every act/skip decision closes within the §10 decision-latency budget. **Deliberation is design-time; execution is reflex:** the "conference room" — multiple analyses arguing a strategy's merits — lives exclusively out-of-band in the §5.6 research loop and §12 prompts (Phronesis-side, between sessions), where it improves *what the reflexes are*, never *whether this tick's reflex fires*.

AGON does NOT know capital, portfolio state, execution, or how to override the NVMA veto.

Concurrent strategy modules (regime-gated): **Trend Following** (dual-EMA cross + RSI filter; conviction = normalised ADX) in trend regimes; **Breakout** (channel break + volume confirm; close-beyond-level false-break filter; conviction = magnitude/ATR) in breakout regimes; **Mean Reversion** (BB extremes + RSI divergence; z-score entry from evolved params; conviction = z × divergence strength) in ranging/mean-reverting regimes; **Momentum Scalp** (tick-velocity surge + directional alignment, 5–15 bar horizon) in high-liquidity trend regimes; **Pair Divergence** (the opposite-movers edge): rolling correlation/spread z-score over historically coupled pairs from `instruments.yaml` (e.g., EURUSD↔USDCHF, AUDUSD↔NZDUSD); entry on divergence beyond evolved z-threshold with coupling-stability confirmation, exit on reconvergence or coupling break; conviction = divergence z × coupling stability; active in ranging/mean-reverting regimes on BOTH legs' instruments. Pair rules: every relationship quotes its correlation window and method (a correlation without its window is a bug); both legs are emitted as one linked signal group so the Governor sizes them together and counts their **net** currency exposure (long EURUSD + short USDCHF ≈ double USD short, not a hedge); a coupling break (rolling correlation crossing its sign/stability floor) while legs are open emits an exit signal for the group — never one leg alone.

Conviction rules (**C4 — order is fixed**): the MEDIUM-volatility ×0.85 uncertainty discount is applied **first**; the < 0.45 discard test is then evaluated against the post-discount conviction (so a raw 0.50 signal in MEDIUM volatility becomes 0.425 and is discarded). LOW-volatility ranging → Trend Following and Breakout modules disabled entirely. Conviction is on a 0–1 scale everywhere — the 0–100 phrasing in the v1.0 architecture brief is retired.

### 5.6 STRATEGY PROVENANCE & THE LITERATURE LAW `[DIR:core/signals]` — anti-self-deception

Strategy modules do not appear from thin air, and backtest numbers are hypotheses, not results.

- **The verified-benchmark law (v2.9, owner-directed):** ACIS measures itself against what *provably* works, not against hype. The canon of systematic edges with decades of published, verifiable evidence — the benchmark ACIS's strategy set must engage with, module by module: (1) **time-series momentum / trend following** at 3–12-month horizons (Moskowitz–Ooi–Pedersen 2012; the century study 1880–2016: Sharpe ≈ 0.4 *every decade*, positive in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns — the backbone of the ~$300B CTA industry). ACIS's momentum modules currently live at short horizons; **a slow multi-month time-series-momentum module is a required canonical implementation for v1** (ACIS-04/ACIS-11), and multi-horizon ensembling is the declared direction. (2) **Volatility scaling/targeting** of positions (verified to raise Sharpe on risk assets and to cut drawdowns/tails broadly; evidence for FX specifically shows the drawdown benefit more than the Sharpe benefit — recorded honestly) — applied in the Governor as the §6 dampen-only vol-normalisation factor. (3) **Fractional-Kelly discipline** — full Kelly is fragile to estimation error; aggregate risk fraction stays deliberately far below it (ACIS's fixed-fractional BASE_UNIT + guardrails already embody this; ACIS-05 records the framing). (4) **Breadth** — the CTA result rests on many uncorrelated markets; the §3.7 multi-asset architecture is the carrier. Un-verified claims (marketing EAs, social-media track records, unaudited "prop signals") are not benchmarks and never enter the canon.
- **Canonical first, raw first.** Every strategy module traces to the recognized trading literature (forex-first, plus the futures/commodities canon as those asset classes enable, §3.7) — books, papers, and published strategies by prominent practitioners (trend-following, mean reversion, breakout, carry, inter-market/pair relationships, news-impact structure). The research loop (LLM-side, via Phronesis, out-of-band per §0): study the source → implement the strategy **faithfully as written** → evaluate it raw under §13/§14 discipline → record the results honestly, especially when poor. Only then may its underlying *reasoning* be used to derive a bespoke variant adapted to today's market structure. The deep spec for `core/signals` names the v1 reading list.
- **Provenance record (mandatory per module):** source citation (author, work, edition/section) or named lineage to a canonical module; the market inefficiency it claims; why that inefficiency should exist in current markets; and **what would falsify it** (a live-performance bound). A strategy that cannot state its falsifier does not enter the Arena. Provenance is exposed on `acis://strategies/provenance` (§12).
- **Declared-deviations law (v2.11, owner-directed):** every provenance record carries `declared_deviations` — an exhaustive list of every departure from the cited source's **actual method** (added stops/targets, changed rebalance cadence, proxy indicators, evolved parameters where the source publishes fixed ones, a different time base). **An empty list is a positive assertion: "implemented as published."** Deviations are exposed on `acis://strategies/provenance` and are a standing question of the external-referent audit; a module found deviating beyond its declaration is dishonest provenance — the exact failure §5.6 exists to prevent (a bespoke variant at birth wearing a canonical citation and borrowing its evidence).
- **Anti-overfitting law (binding, extends §13/§14):** every evaluated variant of a strategy family is counted in an **attempt registry**; reported metrics are read against the number of attempts (you cannot un-try a variant). Parameter plateaus required — a strategy that dies at ±10% on any parameter fails. Win rates and Sharpe from historical data are never quoted without their out-of-sample split and attempt count. **Historical performance alone never promotes a strategy to live:** the path is backtest → paper (live data, no real orders — §12 modes) → live, each rung compared against the previous rung's expectations; drift beyond tolerance drops it back a rung. Live behaviour is the only definition of "works."
- **Expectancy over win rate (binding, with C9).** At every rung a strategy is judged on realised **positive expectancy** and reward-to-risk, never on win rate alone; a win rate is never quoted without its realised R:R and expectancy beside it. The §14 targets deliberately keep *both* — win rate > 48% **and** profit factor > 1.4 — so neither is chased blind. The reference is recognized profitable behaviour, not a flattering number: published trend-following wins ~35–48% of trades yet profits through R:R, while a 90%-win/negative-expectancy curve is a mirage that blows up on its rare losses. "Maximise win rate" is honoured only as "maximise win rate **subject to** positive expectancy."

### 5.7 THE EVOLUTION LAW `[DIR:core/signals + core/optimizer]` — edge is re-earned, never assumed

Markets drift; a strategy that was honest two years ago can be a memory today. QIEA already evolves *parameters*; this section makes **strategy-level** evolution a standing obligation, not an aspiration:

- **Strategy lifecycle (binding):** every Arena module lives on the §5.6 rung ladder its whole life, not just at birth: `incubation (backtest) → paper → live → decayed → retired`. Every strategy and every promoted ParamSet carries a **re-validation due date** (config cadence, default quarterly): a fresh walk-forward on data it has never seen, judged by its own declared falsifier and the C9 expectancy gate. Passing re-arms the date; failing demotes one rung — automatically, logged, no debate. Demotion is risk-reducing and therefore agent-executable; **promotion remains gated as in §5.6** (live behaviour only, human sign-off for live). **Demotion transition (closes GAP-0019, mirrors the QIEA C2 pattern):** a failed re-validation freezes the strategy's **new entries immediately** (instant, risk-reducing); its open positions remain managed by its own exit logic until flat — a live position is never orphaned by its strategy's demotion; removal from the Arena is staged and lands at the next session boundary at which that strategy holds no open positions, with staleness observable exactly like a stalled param promotion.
- **Graveyard with revival conditions:** a retired strategy is never deleted. It enters the graveyard with its full provenance record, final performance, and a **declared revival condition** (typically the regime/inefficiency conditions under which its edge existed). When NVMA regime statistics again match a graveyard entry's revival condition, the system surfaces it on `acis://strategies/provenance` for re-incubation — from the *bottom* rung. Edges are cyclical; the graveyard is a library, not a landfill.
- **Challenger lane:** for any live strategy, bespoke variants (§5.6 derivation) may run concurrently in paper as **challengers** on the same live data. A challenger that beats the champion out-of-sample over the declared comparison window becomes the promotion *candidate* — through the full rung ladder, never by swap-in. Champions are never displaced by backtest numbers.
- **Literature acquisition cadence:** the §5.6 research loop (LLM-side, out-of-band) is a scheduled Phronesis routine, not a one-time seeding: hunt published strategies and books from recognized practitioners → `literature-distil` → faithful implementation → raw evaluation → honest record — continuously growing the incubation pool. The reading-list ledger and each source's disposition (implemented / rejected-with-reason / queued) are part of the provenance store.
- **Nothing here weakens a gate:** re-validation and demotion only ever *remove* capital from a strategy. All §5.6 anti-self-deception machinery (attempt registry, plateaus, rung ladder) applies to every evolved variant, challenger, and revival.

## 6. CAPITAL GOVERNOR `[DIR:core/governor]` — sovereign allocation

The only layer that sees total portfolio state. Signal → SizedOrder.

```
raw_size = BASE_UNIT_USD × conviction × liquidity_factor × correlation_factor
                        × (regime_stability × regime_confidence) × marginal_alpha_factor
size     = apply_hard_limits(raw_size, signal, portfolio)
```

**Marginal-alpha law (anti-greed lever):** estimate d(Alpha)/d(Capital) from rolling 50-trade windows bucketed by size. If marginal ≤ 0 → factor floors at 0.25 (scaling down is mandatory when edge shrinks). Cap 1.5 — never more. **C6 — the normalisation is a contract, not a free parameter:** the map from the raw derivative to the `[0.25, 1.5]` multiplier must be defined and unit-tested in the Governor layer spec, and must satisfy — monotonic non-decreasing in marginal alpha, exactly `0.25` for all marginal ≤ 0, `1.0` at the neutral point (marginal ≈ baseline), never exceeding `1.5`, and never NaN/inf for any finite input. Until enough trades exist to estimate the derivative (config `marginal_alpha_min_trades`), the factor is pinned to `1.0` and the pinned state is logged.

**C3 — regime half-life is advisory, by decision.** `RegimeContext.estimated_half_life_seconds` (§4) is emitted, logged to `regime_log`, and exposed on `acis://regime/current` (§12), but the sizing formula above intentionally folds regime quality into `regime_stability × regime_confidence` and does **not** apply the v1.0 brief's separate `half_life < session_horizon → ×0.5` throttle. Reason: avoid double-counting regime quality and keep the sizing formula's factors independent. Whether to promote half-life to an explicit sizing factor is an open design decision (`SPEC_AUDIT.md`), not a silent omission.

**Volatility normalisation (v2.9, owner-directed — dampen-only, C16):** a `vol_norm_factor ∈ (0, 1]` multiplies `raw_size`: exactly `1.0` while realized volatility (ATR-based, per instrument) is at or below its declared target band, scaling **down** monotonically as realized vol exceeds it. Contract properties (like C6): monotonic non-increasing in excess vol, never > 1.0 (it can only shrink a position — the verified vol-targeting benefit ACIS takes is the drawdown/tail cut, applied risk-reducing-only), `1.0` pinned until the vol estimator is warm (pinned state logged), never NaN/inf. Exact function + target band: `ACIS-05`, unit-tested against these properties.

**Correlation clusters** (config §9): usd_long, usd_short, commodity, yen_carry, risk_on, risk_off. Max 20% of NAV per cluster. Breach → size to zero, never split.

**Hard overrides (size → 0 regardless of formula):** daily drawdown ≥ limit; net single-currency delta > 30%; NVMA `tradeable == False`; regime confidence < 0.55; instrument liquidity below ILLIQUID_THRESHOLD; **instrument inside a platform-rules blackout window (§3.5) on a rule-restricted account — no exception, ever**; account-rule overrides per §9.5 (lot caps, min hold, weekend bans).

**Pre-limit taper (ratified, §9.5):** before the hard overrides evaluate, `raw_size` is additionally multiplied by the pre-limit taper factor delivered by the Risk Monitor (1.0 far from every hard drawdown limit, linearly down to 0 at the entry-halt fraction). The taper arrives over the single upward Risk→Governor edge, exactly like HALT and alpha-decay cuts — **that edge carries only risk-restrictive instructions (halt, cut, taper); it can never raise a size.**

**Linked signal groups (§5.5 Pair Divergence):** the Governor sizes a pair group atomically — both legs or neither; leg sizes balanced so the intended relationship exposure (not two directional bets) is what's on the book; group counted once against its correlation cluster and per-currency **net** delta. If one leg would be blocked by any hard override, the whole group sizes to zero.

## 7. EXECUTION ORCHESTRATOR `[DIR:core/execution]`

Receives SizedOrder; decides only *how and where*, never how much.

Fragmentation: orders > FRAGMENT_THRESHOLD_LOTS split into sub-orders sized `[0.85, 1.15] × (total/n)` uniform-random; inter-fragment jitter uniform `[0.5s, 3.0s]`; configurable minimum fragment (no dust). VWAP over rolling 5-min window for orders > VWAP_THRESHOLD; aggressive IOC market orders for fast-decay signals (valid_for_seconds < 30). Venue routing by live per-instrument quality score (spread, fill rate, slippage), reason logged per order. Signature randomisation: ±15% lot variance, randomised timing, rotated venue order, never identical consecutive patterns. Every fill emits `FillReport{requested_price, fill_price, slippage_bps, fill_time, venue, fragment_id}` to the Risk Monitor.

### 7.5 PLATFORM ADAPTER CONTRACT `[DIR:core/data + core/execution]` — attach fast, attach safely

ACIS connects to trading platforms through **one declared contract**, so that supporting a new broker, prop firm, or futures gateway is an adapter package plus a conformance run — never a change to any core layer. Each platform adapter has two faces split along the layer law: a **feed face** registered in `core/data/feeds.py` (quotes, depth, instrument discovery) and an **execution face** in `core/execution/venue.py` (orders, positions, account state — account state read downstream by the Risk Monitor for reconciliation, per §13). The two faces may share a platform session object owned by the adapter package, **constrained to transport and authentication only (closes GAP-0013): it carries no market, account, or position state** — so it cannot become a runtime upward channel the import-linter can't see. Account/position state flows exclusively through the execution face to the Risk Monitor; the feed face exposes no account or position surface, and the conformance suite asserts this structurally. Neither face ever imports a core layer above its own.

- **Capability descriptor (mandatory):** every adapter declares, as data, what its platform can actually do — order types (market/limit/stop, IOC support), netting vs hedging account model, partial-close support, min lot/contract step, max order size, symbol naming map (the §13 suffix problem is solved *here*, once), quote depth availability, and which asset classes it serves. The Governor and Orchestrator consume the descriptor: a SizedOrder that needs a capability the venue lacks is rejected loudly at the boundary, never emulated silently.
- **Conformance suite (release gate per adapter):** a shared `tests/integration` suite every adapter must pass against its platform's demo/paper environment — round-trip order lifecycle, partial fill, reject, reconnect-mid-position, position reconciliation truth, clock-skew tolerance, symbol-map completeness for the enabled universe. An adapter that hasn't passed conformance cannot be selected in `system_config.yaml`.
- **The attach protocol:** attaching ACIS to a new account = select adapter → discover/verify symbol map and contract metadata against `instruments.yaml` (§3.7) → load the account's `platform_rules.yaml` rulebook (§9.5 — missing rulebook is fatal) → **flat-or-decide (closes GAP-0018): if the account holds open positions, attach halts and requires an explicit human decision at attach time** — flatten them via the platform first, or keep managing them manually until flat; only a flat account proceeds. ACIS never sits in paper mode alongside real positions its guardrails can see but cannot act on, and paper mode is never granted a real-order path — the unguarded window is made unreachable, not patched. Then start in **paper mode always**; live mode still requires the §12 human token and §14 gates. Attach is designed to be *fast* (an evening, not a sprint) precisely because everything platform-specific is declared, not coded.
- **v1 ships** OANDA v20, MT5, and the mock adapter fully conformant. **Adapter order (ratified by the owner, 2026-07-15 — closes GAP-0012):** after the v1 trio: cTrader → DXtrade → TradeLocker → Match-Trader → IBKR → futures gateways (Tradovate/Rithmic-class) — prop-platform reach first, exchange-traded futures last because they carry the extra §3.7 lifecycle risk class. Each is a config + adapter-package addition under this section, not a spec change.
- **Futures live enablement (ratified, with GAP-0012):** futures may be built and paper-traded from phase 1 under the asset-class law (§3.7). A futures account goes **live** only when: all §13 futures glitch classes are green, §14 minimums are met out-of-sample on futures data for that account's universe, the §8.5 scenario replay includes roll/limit-lock scenarios, and a **futures-specific human sign-off** is given for that account. It never piggybacks on a forex sign-off.

## 8. RISK MONITOR `[DIR:core/risk]` — the only upward channel

Continuous `RiskSnapshot`: daily_pnl, daily/open/total drawdown %, 1% VaR, max correlated-cluster %, rolling 50-trade Sharpe and win-rate, alpha_decay_flag, slippage_slope_alert, instruments_halted.

HALT limits live in `config/guardrail_limits.yaml` (version-controlled, human-edited only — §9): daily_drawdown 2.0% (halt all new positions for session) · per-instrument drawdown 1.0% (halt instrument) · alpha-decay Sharpe threshold 0.5× baseline · portfolio 1% VaR cap 3.0% NAV · cluster cap 20% · slippage alert 8.0 bps. **Equity circuit breaker (Master Spec, binding): if account equity drops below the configured floor, kill trading instantly — flatten-and-halt, human required to restart.** (These limits and the equity floor cannot be reached from any sizing or signal path — they are out of reach *structurally*, not by convention: the guard-integrity law, §9.)

Alpha decay: rolling Sharpe(last 50 trades) < threshold × baseline Sharpe(trades −200..−50), minimum 30 trades → flag, instruct Governor to cut all allocations to 25%, log `ALPHA_DECAY_DETECTED`, restore only after confirmed recovery (20 trades with Sharpe > 0.8× baseline). HALT commands go to the Capital Governor ONLY — the single upward edge in the whole system. **C5 — the baseline must be well-defined before the flag can fire:** the baseline window `trades[−200:−50]` requires at least `config.alpha_decay_baseline_min_trades` (default 50) trades actually present in that window; below that the comparison is *inactive* (`alpha_decay_flag` stays False) and the inactive/warming state is surfaced on `acis://risk/snapshot` so "not enough data" can never be silently mistaken for "no decay." This prevents a spurious flag (or a divide-by-near-zero baseline Sharpe) on a short trade history; it does not suppress a real decay signal once the window is populated.

### 8.5 BLACK-SWAN PREPAREDNESS `[DIR:core/risk + config/scenario_book.yaml]`

Surprise is expected; unbounded loss is not.

- `config/scenario_book.yaml`: versioned catalogue of historical and hypothetical shocks with replayable price paths — 2015 CHF de-peg, 2010 flash crash, weekend war-gap, broker outage mid-position, liquidity vacuum at rollover. The book grows by human commit; `halt-postmortem` (§12) proposes additions after every real risk event.
- **Gap law (binding):** sizing and guardrail limits must satisfy max-loss invariants under **gap-through-stop** replay of the scenario book — stops are assumed to fill wherever liquidity reappears, not at their level. Guaranteed-stop assumptions are forbidden unless the platform contractually provides them.
- Standing defences (deterministic, fast-loop): correlation-spike detector (cluster correlations converging toward 1 = risk-off shock ⇒ Governor cuts new sizing to the configured shock fraction); spread-explosion and tick-velocity anomalies already veto via NVMA (§4); broker-connection-loss protocol per §13 (reconcile against broker truth, flatten-and-halt on stalled fast loop with open positions); **Tier-G flatten (§3.6, ratified)** — a two-source-confirmed shock on a `flatten_eligible` taxonomy entry flattens and halts affected instruments.
- Scenario replay runs in `tests/backtest` as a release gate: a build whose replay violates any max-loss invariant does not ship. Replay re-runs whenever the scenario book or guardrail limits change.

### 8.6 THE WATCHDOG (v2.10, owner-ratified) — the breaker must not die with the loop

The equity breaker and HALT machinery live inside the engine process; if that process hangs or dies with open positions, in-process defences die with it. Three independent layers close this, **none of them kernel-level or HFT-grade (lean-build law — a hung Python process is the failure mode, not a hung kernel):**
1. **Broker-side stops (already law):** every `SizedOrder` carries a stop-loss placed AT THE BROKER — position risk is bounded even if ACIS vanishes. The §7.5 conformance suite asserts server-side stop placement per adapter; the §8.5 gap law prices their slippage honestly.
2. **The watchdog process (NEW, phase 9 — ACIS-07/ACIS-12):** a separate, minimal OS process (own interpreter, no imports from `core/`, config read-only) that consumes the engine's heartbeat file/socket. On heartbeat silence beyond `watchdog_timeout_s` with open positions, it flattens via the broker API directly and records `WATCHDOG_FLATTEN` — the same semantics as a stalled-fast-loop flatten (§13), executed from outside the blast radius. It can only ever flatten/halt — it has no sizing, no resume, no config-write capability (guard-integrity: the engine cannot reach the watchdog's config, and the watchdog cannot expand risk). Deep-spec contract, heartbeat format, and tests land in ACIS-12 at phase-9 start; it ships with the VPS deployment (§15 phase 12 readiness).
3. **Out-of-band supervision (already law):** Phronesis may halt/flatten via §12 at any time; platform-side drawdown enforcement (§9.5) is the venue's own backstop.

## 9. CONFIGURATION `[DIR:config]`

`system_config.yaml` (loop cadences, thresholds, BASE_UNIT_USD, adapter/account selection), `instruments.yaml` (universe + per-instrument params + correlation clusters + coupled-pair relationship declarations for §5.5 + **full §3.7 contract metadata and market calendar per instrument**), `guardrail_limits.yaml` (hard limits + equity floor), `platform_rules.yaml` (one rulebook per broker/prop account — the §9.5 account doctrine's source of truth: news-trading permissions and window sizes, min hold times, lot caps, **drawdown limits with their exact measurement semantics** (static vs trailing, intraday vs end-of-day anchor, balance- vs equity-based), consistency/min-trading-day rules, weekend/overnight holding permissions — attach-time fatal if the active account has no rulebook), `event_sources.yaml` (trusted calendar/news channels + trust tiers + Tier-G taxonomy and affected-instrument maps, §3.5–3.6), `scenario_book.yaml` (§8.5). Loaded once at startup via pydantic-settings; schema-validated; a bad config refuses to start (no defaults for risk limits — missing limit = fatal). Guardrail values change only by human commit; the §12 control plane can *read* them, never write them. Runtime-tunable values proposed by LLM/orchestrator go through a staged-promotion path identical to QIEA params.

**The guard-integrity law (binding).** *A guardrail a guarded process can reach is not a guardrail.* ACIS's guardrails do not rest on any module's good behaviour or on a promise not to touch them — they rest on the guarded code being **structurally unable** to reach them, enforced at four independent layers: (1) `acis_stage_config` (§12) rejects any patch touching `guardrail_limits.yaml` or the equity floor **at the schema level** — the path is unrepresentable in the tool, not merely refused at runtime; (2) the host permission layer (Phronesis `ethos` / Claude Code hooks) is deny-by-default on those paths, so a bypassed schema check meets a second wall; (3) the downward-only layer law (§2) and the single upward HALT edge give no code path from a sizing or signal layer up into risk configuration; (4) guardrail values, the equity floor, and HALT/veto semantics change only by human commit. There is no force flag, no "temporary" test hook, and no MCP tool that writes a guardrail. This is the same self-modification-firewall doctrine the sibling engines carry (PROKOPE §10, SYNEDRION §9). If a future change would let any agent, config flag, or test hook weaken a guard, **that change is the bug** — halt and raise it, per CLAUDE.md.

Instrument universe (default; the enabled set is per-account config under §3.7): majors EUR/USD, GBP/USD, USD/JPY, AUD/USD, USD/CAD, NZD/USD, USD/CHF; minors EUR/GBP, EUR/JPY, GBP/JPY, AUD/JPY, EUR/AUD; extensions as adapters and metadata mature: XAU/USD and XAG/USD, index CFDs, WTI, exchange-traded futures (§3.7). An account that only offers XAUUSD is a valid ACIS account — the universe is whatever the attached account tradeably provides, filtered by complete contract metadata. **C7 — cluster membership may reference extension instruments** (e.g. `commodity`/`risk_off` list `XAUUSD_L`) that are not in the default majors/minors set; such references are inert until the instrument is enabled in `instruments.yaml`, and a cluster referencing an unknown symbol is a config error, not a silent drop. Correlation clusters are fixed membership lists (§6), not correlations computed live — the "real-time correlation tracker" measures *exposure within* a cluster, not cluster membership.

### 9.5 THE ACCOUNT DOCTRINE — survive the account you are actually on

A prop account is not a funded account with extra steps; it is a different survival problem. Prop firms fail traders on *their* rules measured *their* way — a trailing drawdown anchored to intraday high-water equity kills an account that a static end-of-day limit would have spared. ACIS therefore treats the attached account's rulebook as a **second, independent set of hard constraints**, composed with its own guardrails:

- **The stricter-of law (binding):** for every constraint that exists in both `guardrail_limits.yaml` and the account's `platform_rules.yaml`, the effective limit is the **stricter** of the two, with the platform's limit evaluated under the platform's own measurement semantics (§9 — static/trailing, intraday/EOD, balance/equity). The Risk Monitor tracks platform-measured drawdown as its own first-class series in `RiskSnapshot`, never approximated by ACIS's internal measure. This only ever *adds* constraints — it can never loosen an ACIS guardrail, and it lives under the same guard-integrity law (§9): rulebooks change by human commit only.
- **Rule enforcement respects the layer law (closes GAP-0014):** platform *drawdown* limits — which require platform-measured account state — are **Risk-Monitor-owned** (layer 7, the only layer that reads platform account state, via the §7.5 execution face) and act on the Governor **over the single upward edge**, exactly like HALT and alpha-decay cuts (§8): the Risk Monitor computes distance-to-limit and the §9.5 taper factor and instructs the Governor; the Governor never computes or reads platform drawdown itself. The remaining rule categories are config-and-events data flowing *downward* and are Governor hard overrides in the ordinary way (two-layer pattern of the §3.5 blackout law): max lot/position caps, weekend/overnight holding bans (positions flattened before the ban window by policy, surfaced if impossible), min-hold-time (no exit before the platform's minimum — factored into strategy eligibility: a strategy whose horizon violates min-hold is not eligible on that account), and news restrictions (§3.5).
- **Consistency rules are eligibility, not pressure (guarantee honesty per GAP-0017):** rules like "no single day > X% of total profit" or "minimum N trading days" are enforced as *constraints on sizing and scheduling* — capping today's remaining risk budget to **minimise the probability** of a consistency breach. Sizing bounds loss, not profit: a single outsized favourable day can still breach a profit-concentration rule, so this is risk reduction, never a guarantee, and the spec claims nothing stronger. Consistency exposure is surfaced continuously on `acis://account/rules`; where the platform permits, early scale-out of an oversized winning day is a per-account, human-ratified policy option, OFF by default. And never pressure: the engine never opens a trade it otherwise wouldn't to satisfy an activity rule; it surfaces the conflict on `acis://status` for the human instead.
- **The pre-limit defence (ratified by the owner, 2026-07-15 — closes GAP-0010, subsumes GAP-0003/F9):** approaching a hard drawdown limit is itself a risk event, not business as usual. For **every** hard drawdown limit in the effective stricter-of set — ACIS's own daily and per-instrument limits and the platform's limits, each measured under its own semantics — the Governor applies a **pre-limit taper**: new-entry size scales linearly from full at `taper_start_fraction` (default **0.60 × limit**) down to **zero at `entry_halt_fraction` (default 0.80 × limit)**; from 0.80 upward, no new position opens on the affected scope (day, instrument, or account, per the limit's own scope). Open positions and their stops are untouched, and the limit itself keeps its existing HALT semantics unchanged at 1.0 — this adds an earlier, softer response in front of every wall; it removes nothing. The fractions live in `guardrail_limits.yaml` (per-account overrides in the `platform_rules.yaml` rulebook schema as `platform_limit_buffer`), human-committed under the guard-integrity law; the defaults above are the ratified floor — an account may be stricter, never looser. Distance-to-limit and the active taper factor per limit are surfaced on `acis://account/rules` and in `RiskSnapshot`.
- **Rulebook attestation (harness awareness, with §12):** the active rulebook carries a content hash exposed on `acis://account/rules`; going live requires acknowledging it (§12 `acis_set_mode`), and the supervising harness re-verifies it at attach, session start, and before any resume proposal. An account whose platform forbids news trading is therefore un-tradeable-through-news at three independent levels: the events layer computes the windows (§3.5), the Governor hard-overrides them (§6), and no harness can arm the system without attesting to the rule's existence.
- Personally funded accounts are the degenerate case: an empty-of-restrictions rulebook that still declares its measurement semantics. The doctrine costs nothing there and saves the account everywhere else.

## 10. THREE-SPEED LOOP ARCHITECTURE

Three independent asyncio loops sharing read access to stores, zero synchronous coupling:
- **Loop 1 SLOW (4–24h):** QIEA evolution at session boundaries with zero open positions; stages, never promotes mid-session.
- **Loop 2 MEDIUM (60–300s):** NVMA classification → `regime_store`; handles shift events.
- **Loop 3 FAST (tick–5s):** stream MarketState → check `regime.tradeable` → fetch active params → AGON → Governor → Execution.

Isolation rules: Loop 1 cannot read live portfolio state; Loop 2 cannot trigger execution; Loop 3 cannot mutate params. Each loop heartbeats to `monitoring/` (§12); a stalled loop is a reportable health event, and a stalled Loop 3 with open positions triggers the Risk Monitor's flatten-and-halt.

**Decisiveness (binding — the anti-paralysis rule).** Survival is the first priority; *inertia is not survival.* When the fast loop holds a `tradeable` regime, an unexpired `Signal`, and a non-zero Governor size, it must reach an act/skip decision within a bounded **decision-latency budget** (config `fast_loop_decision_budget_ms`; the concrete value is owned by the deep spec). The layered vetoes, the HALT/veto priority (§2), the equity breaker, and the `ambiguity = no trade` rule stay **sovereign and are never softened** — but the conservative defaults *around* them may not be tuned so tight that the engine structurally never trades. A system that loses no money and makes no money has failed (§0, Final Mandate). To keep starvation visible rather than silent, the fast loop counts **signals-considered vs trades-taken** and surfaces the ratio on `acis://status` (§12); a persistently near-zero take-rate is a reportable health condition, exactly like a stalled loop — decisiveness is measured, not assumed. **Starvation must also be diagnosable, not just visible:** every skip is attributed to the *specific* gate that killed it (NVMA veto reason, conviction discard, cluster cap, hard override, expiry, blackout, account rule §9.5), and the per-gate skip counters ride alongside the take-rate on `acis://status`. When the engine isn't trading, one glance answers *which* hand is on its throat — so the response is a targeted staged-config proposal (or the honest conclusion that the market offered nothing), never a blind loosening of everything.

## 18. BUILD DOCTRINE (binding, owner-directed)

Owner directive (2026-07-15, in force immediately, through the end of the build, and always): *build ruthlessly on what works without re-inventing and learning from scratch — without compromising the spec's principles or fixating on one channel; the engine must stay dynamic — and avoid scope creep, over-engineering, and complexity that only raises future maintenance cost with no benefit.* Two named laws:

**The proven-parts law (binding).** Build ruthlessly on what already works: the §16 stack's mature libraries, established algorithms and patterns from the recognized literature (the §5.6 canonical-first law is the same principle applied to strategies), and existing well-tested code in this repo. Never re-implement what a proven dependency or an existing module already does well — a hand-rolled indicator library, backtester, or scheduler where §16 already names one is a defect. Three hard boundaries: (1) no new dependency without the §16 gate ("nothing without a spec edit"; adapter-tier SDKs per the §16 exception only); (2) no single-vendor or single-channel fixation — the §7.5 adapter contract and §3.7 declared-data patterns are the model: capability lives behind a declared contract, never hardcoded to one provider, feed, or platform; (3) adopting a proven part never overrides a spec principle — the layer law (§2), guard-integrity (§9), the LLM boundary (§0), and fast-loop determinism bend for no library, however battle-tested. Staying dynamic is part of the law, not in tension with it: what "works" is re-validated on the standing cadence, never enshrined (§5.7 evolution law) — a proven part is proven *until the evidence says otherwise*.

**The lean-build law (binding).** Scope creep, over-engineering, and speculative complexity are defects, not diligence. This extends §17's anti-overengineering rules from design into every build phase: implement exactly what the current §15 phase's spec section plus its deep spec require — no more; no abstractions for hypothetical futures (the declared contracts in §3.7 and §7.5 already carry the future — new instruments, platforms, and strategies attach through them, not through pre-built generality); prefer deleting code to adding it; every added module, class, and dependency must name the spec requirement it serves, or it does not merge. Maintenance cost is a first-class design input: a clever construct that the next reader must decode is more expensive than the plain one that works.

Both laws bind the implementing agent **and** every future spec pass. Neither may ever be cited to weaken a guardrail, skip a test gate, or bypass the §5.6/§13/§14 validation discipline — "proven elsewhere" is not "validated here": a mature library's output still crosses the same frozen-dataclass boundaries, passes the same glitch-class tests, and clears the same out-of-sample gates as anything hand-written.

## FINAL ENGINEERING MANDATE

> The system's job is not to make the most money possible on any given day. Its job is to **still be running, with positive expectancy, two years from now.** Every design decision must answer: *"Does this make the system more likely to survive a regime it has never seen?"* If the answer is no — redesign it.
